Brands Hatch GP rewards commitment, but the lap is easy to overdrive. Coach
the driver to set the car early, respect the elevation changes, and feed
throttle in progressively when the car is light or off-camber. Most time comes
from clean exits out of Druids, Surtees, Westfield, Stirlings, and Clark Curve.

## Paddock Hill Bend

Start from the left side of the road and brake just after the 2 board while
beginning a slight turn-in. Aim for a late apex because the corner falls away
and stays blind on entry. Downshift to third, add steering as the inside
barrier comes into view, touch the inside kerb with the front-right, then feed
the throttle back in smoothly and use the exit kerb without snapping the car
wide.

## Druids

Move fully left on the climb and use the marshal post on the outside barrier as
the braking reference. Brake hard from fourth to first, turn in earlier than it
feels natural, and stay close to the inside kerb for a long apex. Avoid climbing
too much kerb because it unsettles the car. Wait until the exit opens before
asking for real throttle, then unwind the wheel and use the exit kerb without
running beyond it.

## Graham Hill Bend

After Druids, change direction quickly and keep right for the downhill left.
Brake in a straight line just before the outside kerbing begins, shift to
second, and commit to a mid-corner apex. The priority is momentum and early
power, but do not cut the inside kerb because it can trigger TC or rotate the
car. Use the exit kerb and matting while keeping the inside wheels within track
limits.

## Surtees

Stay right and brake just after the 1 board while trail braking into the
off-camber left. Shift to second, first aim the car toward the middle of the
road, then add steering for an apex after the inside kerb changes from double
to single. The entry is bumpy near the apex, so keep the car settled, hug the
inside until the kerb thins, and prioritize the exit onto the following
straight. Too much exit kerb will hurt traction and cost speed all the way up
the road.

## Hawthorn Bend

Approach from the left and brake just before the 1 board, carrying trail brake
to the inside. Use fourth or third depending on the car. Turn in early enough to
stay tight, then get back to power as soon as the car accepts it. Let the car
run naturally to the exit kerb, but keep track-limit margin because a small
dust kick can become an invalid lap.

## Westfield Bend

Approach from the left, brake hard at the 1 board, and shift to third. Trail
brake into the right-hander and use the inside kerb aggressively enough that
the red-and-white kerb is near the outside wheels at apex. Once the car is
pointed uphill, return to throttle and use all available exit kerb and matting.
This is a corner where testing track-limit margin is worth lap time.

## Sheene Curve

This blind uphill right needs commitment without panic. From the left, use the
inside 1 board as the gentle braking cue, begin turning as you brake, and shift
to third. Cut the inside kerb with light trail brake, then return to throttle
just after apex. There is no real exit kerb, so do not chase extra speed wide
onto the grass.

## Stirlings

Move back to the right after Sheene and brake just before the 1 board. Do not
try to win the corner on the brakes; set up the exit instead. Shift to second,
use the banking, and keep a small trail brake phase while placing the left
front over the inside kerb. Get on throttle as early as the banking allows, but
watch cars that bottom out on the exit kerb because it can drag the car wide.

## Clark Curve

Approach from the left and brake gently at the 1 board, then shift to third.
Turn in earlier than instinct suggests and target the midpoint of the inside
kerb. Trail brake smoothly to help rotation, then feed throttle in just before
apex. The exit looks generous but disappears if mid-corner speed is too high;
avoid throttle understeer and let the car drift right toward the pit wall for
the shortest run to the line.
