# Kestrel — Analysis Results Tab

Approximate runtime: 3 minutes  
Format: Screen walkthrough with first-person voice-over

## Before recording

[Prepare a live session with at least three analyzed laps from the same car and track, valid lap times, and a result with an available Driver vs Expert comparison. Use the actual labels and values shown in the recording.]

## Opening: from lap time to driving behavior

[Open Analysis Results. Briefly show Overall Trends, then switch to Lap Results.]

This is Kestrel's Analysis Results tab. Once my laps have been analyzed, this is where I come to understand what happened and decide what to work on next.

A lap time tells me how fast I went. Here, I can look at individual sections of the lap, compare my driving with an expert reference, and see how my results change over several laps.

## Find a lap and read the results

[Show the track, car, and lap number beside the page controls. Use Previous or Next to move between analyzed laps, then settle on the demonstration lap.]

In Lap Results, each page contains the analysis for a baseline lap. I can move between them here and check which car, track, and lap I'm reviewing.

[Select All results in the View menu. Point to the frequency chart, then scroll to a numbered result card.]

The chart shows how often each label appears in the current view. Below it, the numbered cards show the individual results, with labels and, where available, the track section and position range. That gives me a specific part of the lap to investigate.

## Choose what to focus on

[Change View to Mistakes, then Most common label in mistakes. Keep the reordered cards visible.]

I can narrow the view to mistakes, then bring the ones with the most common mistake labels to the top. This helps me spot repeated habits and choose a practice focus.

[Show Most time lost in mistakes. If the demonstration data contains time-gap values, point to a ranked result and its Time gap field; otherwise, briefly show the menu option without claiming a measured loss.]

There's also a view for the most time lost in mistakes. When time-gap data is available, it ranks the results by the recorded gap change, so I can investigate the larger losses first.

## Watch the Driver vs Expert comparison

[Hover over a card that says Hover or focus for Driver and Expert comparison. Hold the pointer there while the replay runs.]

When a result has comparison data, hovering over it opens this animated replay of my driving alongside the expert reference.

[Show the trajectories and the available throttle, brake, and gear readouts. Leave a few seconds without narration so viewers can follow the movement.]

I can follow the two lines through the section and compare the available driving inputs as they play back. This gives me a clearer picture of where my approach differs and what I could try changing on the next lap.

## Look for progress across laps

[Switch to Overall Trends. Show Lap Time Improvement, including Actual lap time and Best-fit trend.]

Overall Trends brings together the analyzed laps saved in this session. The lap-time chart shows my actual times and a trend line, so I can look beyond one quick lap.

[Scroll to Overall Mistake Trend. Show the Training Mistake and Racing Mistake choices, then choose a category with populated results. Use Specific mistake to select an observed mistake.]

That lets me check whether the habit I'm practicing is appearing less often, alongside any change in pace.

## Closing

[Return to the chosen lap and hover over the result used in the demonstration. End on its comparison replay.]

That's how I use the Analysis Results tab: find a pattern, inspect the driving behind it, and choose one thing to practice. After another few laps, I can come back and see what changed.
